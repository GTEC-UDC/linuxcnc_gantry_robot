# Inverse Kinematics

The `calibxyzkins` module uses the Newton-Raphson method to solve the inverse kinematics problem iteratively. Given desired axis positions $(x', y', z')$, the module finds the corresponding joint positions $(x, y, z)$ that satisfy the transformation equation.

## Problem Statement

The inverse kinematics problem is to solve for $(x, y, z)$ given $(x', y', z')$ in the equation:

$$\mathbf{f}(x, y, z) = A \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} + B \begin{bmatrix} x^2 \\ y^2 \\ z^2 \end{bmatrix} + C = \begin{bmatrix} x' \\ y' \\ z' \end{bmatrix}$$

This is equivalent to finding the roots of:

$$\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = A \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} + B \begin{bmatrix} x^2 \\ y^2 \\ z^2 \end{bmatrix} + C - \begin{bmatrix} x' \\ y' \\ z' \end{bmatrix} = 0$$

## Newton-Raphson Algorithm

The Newton-Raphson method iteratively refines the solution using:

$$\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix}_{n+1} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix}_n - J^{-1} \mathbf{F}(x_n, y_n, z_n)$$

where $J$ is the Jacobian matrix of the transformation function.

## Jacobian Matrix

The Jacobian matrix is computed as:

$$J = \frac{\partial \mathbf{F}}{\partial [x, y, z]} = A + 2 \cdot \text{diag}(x, y, z) \cdot B$$

Expanding this:

$$J = \begin{bmatrix}
a_{11} + 2b_{11}x & a_{12} + 2b_{12}y & a_{13} + 2b_{13}z \\
a_{21} + 2b_{21}x & a_{22} + 2b_{22}y & a_{23} + 2b_{23}z \\
a_{31} + 2b_{31}x & a_{32} + 2b_{32}y & a_{33} + 2b_{33}z
\end{bmatrix}$$

## Algorithm Steps

The inverse kinematics solver follows these steps:

1. **Initialize joint positions**: Start with the previous solution or bounded initialization if joint limits are specified.

2. **Compute function value**: Calculate $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z)$ using current joint positions.

3. **Check convergence**: If $\|\mathbf{F}\| < \text{tolerance}$, the solution has converged.

4. **Compute Jacobian**: Calculate $J$ at the current joint positions.

5. **Solve linear system**: Compute $\Delta = J^{-1} \mathbf{F}$.

6. **Update joints**: Set $\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} - \Delta$.

7. **Apply constraints**: Ensure joint positions remain within specified limits.

8. **Check iteration limit**: If maximum iterations reached, report convergence failure.

9. **Repeat**: Go to step 2 until convergence or failure.

## Solver Parameters

The solver behavior is controlled by two HAL pins:

- **`calibxyzkins.max-iter`** (default: 10): Maximum number of iterations
- **`calibxyzkins.tol`** (default: 1e-3): Convergence tolerance for $\|\mathbf{F}\|$

## Joint Limits

Optional joint limits can be specified to constrain the solution:

- **`calibxyzkins.min-limit.x/y/z`**: Minimum joint positions  
- **`calibxyzkins.max-limit.x/y/z`**: Maximum joint positions

When limits are specified:
- Initial guess is bounded within the limits
- Solution is clamped to the limits after each iteration
- This helps prevent the solver from diverging to unrealistic positions

## Convergence Considerations

### Well-Conditioned Cases
For small calibration corrections (typical case):
- The Jacobian is close to the identity matrix
- Convergence is typically achieved in 2-3 iterations
- Default tolerance of 1e-3 is usually sufficient

### Challenging Cases
For larger corrections or poorly conditioned systems:
- May require more iterations (`max-iter` > 10)
- Tighter tolerance may be needed (`tol` < 1e-3)
- Joint limits become more important to prevent divergence

### Convergence Failure
If the solver fails to converge:
- Check that calibration parameters are valid
- Verify that the desired position is achievable
- Consider relaxing the tolerance or increasing iteration limit
- Ensure joint limits are reasonable if specified

## Debugging

Verbose output of the inverse kinematics solver can be enabled by compiling the code with the `CALIB_XYZ_INVERSE_PRINT_ITER` macro defined. This will print:
- Iteration number
- Current joint positions
- Function value and norm
- Jacobian matrix
- Update step

This debugging information is useful for understanding convergence behavior and diagnosing problems with specific calibration parameter sets.
